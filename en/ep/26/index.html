Ep. 26 “Every Umbrella Is Mine” — English script

Page 1

November. Cold rain over Nashigasaka City Hall.

Nice umbrella. A wooden handle sits right in the hand.

...Whose umbrella is that?

Dunno. The best one in the umbrella stand.

That is called stealing!!

I only borrowed it. Umbrellas go around, like they say.

Note: A twist on the Japanese proverb "money goes around in the world" (kane wa tenka no mawarimono).

That saying is about money.

Page 2

General Affairs. We need a fix for the umbrella mix-ups.

Thirty this month. That is a fail.

Note: Ms. Kinugawa of General Affairs grades everything with a circle (pass), a cross (fail), or a triangle (half marks), the marks used on Japanese school tests.

Ah... my umbrella.

..........

No. 31. Fail.

Give it back!!

I said I only borrowed it.

The Special Trends Unit will take this one on.

Page 3

Solved!!

Write your name on it, and mix-ups drop to zero.

Grown-ups will not write it.

Too embarrassing.

I do write mine.

What are you, a schoolkid?

My mother taught me to.

...Only the chief has written his.

The poster. Half marks.

Page 4

My mother gave me this umbrella when I got the job.

If I lost it, I could not face her.

..........

Keep it on. Now even I cannot mix it up.

Thank you so much!!

I just hate the noise when people cry!!

Page 5

Which one is my umbrella?

The next morning.

Found it!!

Hey, that is nice.

Where do they sell those?

From Mr. Genda, in the basement...

...Um, the umbrella thief?

Page 6

One marker, please.

Three.

Name your price!!

..........

...There is money in this.

Page 7

Leftovers from last year's Pear Festival.

Three hundred of them. I was told to throw them out.

Marker charms are a big trend, you know.

Note: Small charms tied to umbrellas and bottles so that you can tell yours apart. In the story they top this year's hit-product ranking.

Number one hit product of the year.

300 yen each. Zero cost to me!!

My commission is...

Ten yen.

Twenty.

...Fifteen.

Stop right there!! No selling things inside City Hall!

If the mix-ups go down, that is a pass.

...It is a pass?

Page 8

Thanks! The Nashigasaka marker, 300 yen!!

That makes sixty sold.

...Wait a second.

They are all the same pear.

If they are all the same, they mark nothing!!

Got a pear? It is a staff umbrella.

No pear? It is a visitor's.

Page 9

...So nobody takes a visitor's umbrella.

And staff are all on the same City Hall team.

If they are all the same, it is not a mix-up. It is a swap!!

That is what a mix-up is...

Page 10

Mix-up reports: zero. That is a pass.

They all have a pear, so any one will do, right?

Three days later.

See that, Akamine!!

Losing to him on numbers makes me so mad...!

You put one on yours too, Rena.

I-it is a 12,000-yen umbrella.

Just in case. Just in case!

I want one for my teacup too.

...So it is not just umbrellas, huh.

Page 11

In one week, City Hall turned into pears.

...Which one is mine?

The one with a pear on it.

That is all of them!!

Tea is tea, whatever cup it is in.

Hey! That is mine!!

Two hundred eighty. Twenty left.

Page 12

Three hundred, sold out!!

That is 90,000 yen.

Everyone in City Hall has the same pear...

It has a pear. All right, swap.

Page 13

It has started raining.

There will be some in the stand.

Friday evening. The morning had been sunny.

That stand holds a hundred. There will be spares.

...Are there enough?

Page 14

It has a pear! It is mine!!

They all have one...

My umbrella!! 12,000 yen!!

Mine is a folding one.

So I will be heading home.

Page 15

I put a pear on my umbrella too, but...

...this one has a pear as well.

..........

Umbrellas taken home this week: one hundred.

Umbrellas brought in: nine. That is a fail.

Page 16

Monday morning.

Ch-Chief. That was, um...

a swap, you see...

Pine. You have a name tag, I see.

Note: The chief is talking to his bonsai pine. The pot has a small tag that reads "Pine".

A marker works because it differs from the rest.

Eek.

I told you. Write your name on it.

You had a pear on yours, though, Rena.

Page 17

Refund every yen you took.

And return each umbrella to its owner, one by one.

A hundred!? When nobody knows whose is whose!?

Indeed. They all have the same pear.

That is not mine.

Mine was newer.

A hundred umbrellas came back to the stand.

Name stickers. Five yen each, and I write it for you.

You are charging for that!?

There you are... my 12,000 yen...

Page 18

Last one. Probably yours.

Eight at night. Umbrella number one hundred.

...My name.

Thank you so much, Mr. Genda!!

Chief's orders. Thank the chief.

I have not been paid five yen for that sticker.

Page 19

...So where is my umbrella?

You have never brought one. Not once.

There is one left!!

Umbrellas go around, as they say.

The schoolkid wins.

Note: The last umbrella in the stand carries a name tag: Gotokuji, the chief. Tetsu had mocked him for writing his name "like a schoolkid".
