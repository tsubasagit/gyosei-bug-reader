Page 1

Hand warmers, ten yen each.

Page 2

Trends? That's my unit.

A win for Tourism... it'll reach the main building...!

November 15th is Kimono Day, after all.

Note: Kimono Day (November 15) was started by the kimono industry to get people wearing kimono; it falls in the Shichi-Go-San season.

Page 3

My husband started it. I want this year to be the last.

A hundred signed up. But we're short on kimono and on dressers.

Kimono dressing... that's spinning the sash around, right?

Note: Kitsuke (kimono dressing) is a skill of its own: layers, cords and the obi sash go on in a set order, and many people hire a dresser for formal occasions.

Page 4

Nashigasaka Shopping Street. Kimono shop "Matsunoya."

Wh-what's that stink...

Camphor. It keeps the moths away.

Rather than look, why not try one on?

I'll do it! For Tourism's track record!

Page 5

First, underwear and tabi socks. Then the under-kimono.

Pull the collar back from the nape, one fist's width.

Right side first, left on top. That's "right-front."

Note: "Right-front" (migi-mae) means the right panel goes on first, under the left. Everyone, men and women, wears kimono this way.

Right-front...

So your right hand can slip inside.

Ooh, Akamine in a kimono...

Page 8

So, uh... how much does a kimono cost?

A silk visiting kimono, tailored, runs hundreds of thousands of yen.

Note: A homongi ("visiting kimono") is a semi-formal silk kimono worn to weddings, ceremonies and parties.

H-hundreds of thousands!?

But fewer people wear them. Sales are a tenth of what they were at the peak.

Kimono retail market: 1981 approx. 1.8 trillion yen -> 2025: 203 billion yen (Yano Research Institute)

Every home has unworn kimono asleep in a chest.

...My parents' place has some.

But tourist rentals cost 3,000-8,000 yen a day, with lines!
