Ep. 7 “These Stairs Are for Sale” — English script

Page 1

Rainy day, no field work... this is heaven~

No sleeping on the job!!

Rain or shine, same hourly rate.

Autumn rains. Nashigasaka City Hall, the basement.

She's from the choir I go to hear every year...

The hall we've sung in for forty years... it leaks...

Wh-what the—?!

The ceiling's all stained...?!

Page 2

Umbrellas INSIDE the hall?!

At this rate, next month's concert...

Repairs that aren't in the budget can't happen fast...

We'll fix it... I promise...!!

...We have no money for it.

Page 3

Chief, don't make promises if there's no money!

Chief, you have no plan.

Need money? Naming rights!!

Note: Many Japanese cities sell sponsors the right to name stadiums and halls for a fixed term and yearly fee.

Sell the name to a company, and that pays for the repairs!!

Does Nashigasaka do that?

Not yet!! So it'd be Nashigasaka's first big win!!

People pay money... for a NAME...?!

Page 4

Stuff nobody's named yet...

STAIRS!!

TOILETS!!

BENCHES!!

I can sell ALL of it!!

Page 5

Naming fees go to the repairs!!

The broker's fee goes in MY pocket!!

No commission? Then I clock out on time.

You can't just sell off the names of city facilities!!

...But a Nashigasaka first...

Then I'll find names I CAN sell!!

Well, it's quitting time. See you.

Page 6

Raaagh!! I'll read EVERY ordinance!!

—That night.

Facility names are set by ordinance...!!

Note: The official name of a public facility is written into a city ordinance, so only a council vote can change it. That's why cities sell “nicknames” instead.

Can't just change 'em...!!

Wait... what about a “nickname” the ordinance doesn't cover?

—FOUND IT!! Nicknames, I can sell!!

Slap on as many nicknames as you like — who's it gonna hurt!!

Page 7

From that day on, Tetsu raced to every company in Nashigasaka.

Your company's name — on the stairs!!

Stairs, eh... SOLD!!

Your bakery's name — on a bench!!

Sit down, think bread... SOLD!!

Your orchard's name — on the toilets!!

T-toilets... ugh, fine, SOLD!!

Thank you for your business!!

Thirty deals today alone...

...How is he even closing these...?

Page 8

Company name plates went up all over City Hall.

E-even on THIS...?!

...Still, thirty deals...!

There are more plates than notices...

...Where'd the counter numbers go...?

We can hold our concert...!!

And the fees are rolling in!!

Page 9

There's nowhere left to sell inside City Hall.

Then we go outside!! Sidewalks, plazas, the hall's seats!!

More results... no reason to stop him...!

Kid, you get a commission on every contract!!

You should've said that first!!

Our HQ's in the next town, but if my name's up there!!

“Front” of what? Your office isn't here.

Note: Japanese bus stops are often named “(landmark)-mae” — “in front of (landmark)” — after whatever stands beside them.

It's a nickname, so it's fine!!

Page 10

A man's collapsed at Marumaru Works Front!!

We can't locate a place by that name!!

Do you know the address?!

This spot has FOUR names...!!

Note: The signs read “Midori Plaza,” “Kibo-no-Oka (Hope Hill)” and “Smile Terrace” — plus the new “Marumaru Works Front.”

The ambulance kept circling, searching for the spot.

Page 11

The man who collapsed is safe... yes.

The corporate nicknames... I am truly sorry.

The fire department called City Hall about it.

O-one plate at a time...!!

Mr. Genda, your desk gets a nickname too.

Note: His old plate read “Genda Tetsu's Desk.” The new one reads “City Hall Volunteer Officer.”

Your fees will be refunded in full.

Page 12

We can sing without umbrellas!!

A month later. The civic hall's leak was fixed.

Zero... entries...

Only bench naming rights became an official program.

I'm here to apply!!

Forget a name. Just put this place's address on it!!

That's just an address sign!!

Ambulances will love it.
